Black Friday 2026 is November 27th. From today that's twelve weeks.

Twelve weeks sounds like plenty. It isn't, because the useful window is shorter than the calendar window. Anything you change in the second week of November has no data behind it and no time to fix if it goes wrong. Anything you change in September gets tested by two months of ordinary traffic first, which is the whole point.

So here's what I'd actually do with the twelve weeks. None of it is a rebuild. Most of it is about a week of work.

## 1. Your product page is doing three jobs

Open your best selling product on a phone. Before you scroll, count what the page is trying to do.

Most of the ones I open are doing three things at once. Selling this product, explaining the brand, and pushing four other products. All above the fold, all before the visitor has moved a finger.

Pick one job. This page sells this product. Above the fold that means the product image, the name, the price, the variant picker, and the button. Nothing else. The brand story, the "you may also like" carousel, the reviews widget and the shipping banner all still exist. They just move below the point where the visitor has already decided.

Cross sell is not free. Every extra option above the fold is a decision you've asked someone to make instead of buying. In November, when they've arrived from an ad, on a phone, with fourteen other tabs open, that's an expensive thing to ask.

I wrote about this properly in [Your Shopify Product Page Is Doing Three Jobs](https://rohankewal.dev/blog/shopify-product-page-above-the-fold). If you only fix one thing on this list, fix that one.

## 2. Everything lives in one collection

A lot of the stores I open have one real collection, called something like "All Products" or "Shop", holding ninety items in the order they were added.

That's not a catalogue. That's a warehouse with the doors open.

Collections are a merchandising decision, not a settings screen. The questions worth answering:

- **What are people actually shopping for?** Not your internal product categories. The words a customer would use. "Gifts under £50" is a collection. "SKU Group B" is not.
- **What's the gift collection?** In Q4 a lot of your traffic is buying for someone else and doesn't know your range. Give them somewhere to land.
- **Do your filters work on a phone?** Size, colour, price. If someone has to scroll ninety products to find theirs, they won't. They leave, and it shows up in your data as a bounce and nothing more.

This is an afternoon of work, and it's the afternoon with the most leverage on this list, because collection pages are where paid traffic lands.

## 3. Every app is loading on every page

Reviews, upsells, a popup, a currency switcher, a chat widget, a wishlist, a size guide, a back in stock notifier. Each one brings its own JavaScript, its own stylesheet, sometimes its own font. By default most of them load on every template, whether or not the feature appears on that page.

The reviews app needs to be on the product page. It does not need to be on the cart. The popup does not need to be on checkout. The size guide does not need to be on your blog.

Two things to do:

- **Open your app list and delete the dead ones.** Not disable. Delete, then check the theme for the snippets the uninstall left behind, because they don't always go on their own. Most stores I look at are paying for at least one app nobody has opened in a year.
- **Restrict the survivors to the templates that use them.** Most modern apps allow this through app embeds or theme app extensions. The ones that don't are worth reconsidering.

The test isn't your desktop on office wifi. It's a phone on hotel wifi in November, which is the actual condition your Black Friday traffic will be deciding under.

## 4. Two of your three email flows don't exist

Almost every store I open has abandoned cart switched on. Not many have the other two.

- **Abandoned cart.** You have this one. Check the timing and check it renders on a phone. That's usually enough.
- **Browse abandonment.** Someone looked at a product three times and didn't add it to the cart. In Q4 that's often a person deciding on a gift, and one email at the right moment gives them a reason to come back. Most of the stores I open don't have this at all.
- **Post purchase.** The highest intent audience you have is someone who bought from you eleven seconds ago. In December they're still shopping for gifts. If your post purchase sequence is a shipping confirmation and nothing else, that's the easiest thing on this list to fix.

None of these are hard. They're an afternoon each in whatever email tool you already pay for. They get skipped because they're invisible. Nobody looks at their store and thinks the flows look wrong.

## 5. Nobody has looked at the reporting

This is the one that gets left until November, and it's the one that decides the other four.

In about twelve weeks you're going to discount something. Do you know which collection actually carries margin? Which products get bought together? Which channel brought last year's best customers, as opposed to the most customers?

If the answer to any of those is a shrug, your Black Friday plan is 20% off sitewide and hope. That's how a store ends up with its busiest ever week and its worst ever margin.

Shopify's built in analytics will answer more of this than people expect. Where they don't, a reporting dashboard isn't a big project. It's a few days of connecting up data you already have into something you can read on the morning of the 27th.

Do this first, in September. It tells you which products deserve the work in items 1 and 2, and it stops you discounting the thing that was already selling.

## What twelve weeks actually looks like

- **September.** Reporting first, then collections and filters. These are the decisions everything else depends on.
- **October.** Product page cleanup, app audit, speed pass. Then let it run on normal traffic for a few weeks, so anything you broke surfaces while it's still cheap to fix.
- **Early November.** Email flows, one more pass on a phone, then stop. Nothing structural after the second week.
- **Late November.** Watch. Don't build.

## The part nobody says out loud

The reason a store isn't ready in November usually isn't that the owner didn't care. It's that all five of these are invisible. None of them look like anything. You can't screenshot a properly restricted app load or a working browse abandonment flow, and none of them scratch the itch that a new theme scratches.

The thing that does look like progress, a redesign, is the one thing I wouldn't be doing twelve weeks out.

September is the boring month. That's what makes it the useful one.
